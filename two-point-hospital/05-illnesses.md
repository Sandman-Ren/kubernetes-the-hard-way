# Two Point Hospital — Illness Compendium

A complete reference to every illness in Two Point Hospital (Two Point Studios, 2018), covering the base game and all seven major expansions. Data is parsed from the infoboxes of all 278 illness pages on the Two Point Hospital Wiki (Fandom), cross-checked against the GameFAQs strategy guide.

## How illnesses work

### Visual vs non-visual illnesses

Every patient arrives with exactly one illness, and every illness is classed as **visual** (91 of the 278) or **non-visual** (187):

- **Visual illnesses** physically transform the patient — a light bulb for a head (Lightheadedness), full clown regalia (Jest Infection), Freddie Mercury possession (Mock Star), or an all-over Egyptian wrap (Premature Mummification). You can spot them in the corridor before a single test is run. Visual illnesses are the game's comedic signature, and most of them are cured in a bespoke machine room built just for them.
- **Non-visual illnesses** look like ordinary (grumbling, vomiting, collapsing) patients and only reveal themselves through diagnosis. Almost everything treated in the Pharmacy, Ward, Injection Room, Surgery, Fracture Ward and DNA Lab is non-visual.

### Diagnosis difficulty

Each illness has a hidden **diagnosis difficulty** percentage (0%–90% in the data below). It governs how much of the diagnosis bar each visit fills: a 0% illness like Grout is usually identified in one GP visit, while an 80% illness like Shattered, Touch of Midas or Spontaneous Combustion may bounce through the GP's Office, General Diagnosis, Cardiology, M.E.G.A Scan and more before the bar reaches the confidence threshold. Higher-difficulty illnesses therefore clog your diagnosis chain and are the main reason to invest in staff with high Diagnosis skill and top-tier diagnosis rooms. Each illness also has a preferred pecking order of diagnosis rooms (recorded on the wiki as "best rooms"); the GP's Office is the hub that every patient returns to between tests.

### Severity, health and cure chance

Patients tick down in **health** while they wait; some illnesses (e.g. Mood Poisoning) deteriorate faster than others, and a patient who hits zero dies — possibly returning as a ghost for your Janitors to capture. The eventual **cure chance** depends on diagnosis certainty, the treatment room's prestige and upgrade level, and the treating staffer's skill — not on the illness alone — so even a 0% difficulty illness can be fumbled by an unqualified junior in a shabby room. Each cure also pays a fixed **list price** (shown below), which rises with your reputation and marketing.

### Treatment categories

Every illness maps to exactly one treatment room. The rooms fall into three broad families:

| Category | Rooms | Staffing | Illnesses cured |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-illness treatment rooms | Pharmacy (41), Ward (37), Injection Room (33), Fracture Ward (31) | Nurse | 142 |
| Qualified-specialist rooms | Psychiatry (36), Surgery (33), DNA Lab (29) | Doctor with the matching qualification (Surgery also needs a Nurse) | 98 |
| Machine / specialty clinics | 30 one-off rooms, from the base game's De-Lux Clinic and Pans Lab to DLC rooms like Cryptology, the Clown Clinic and Toad Hall | Doctor or Nurse at a bespoke machine | 38 |

The machine clinics are the money-makers: each cures one illness family (usually visual), must be researched or unlocked on a specific level, and charges the highest prices in the game.

### Contagion and epidemics

A handful of illnesses arrive as **epidemics**: an infected patient wanders your hospital secretly spreading the illness (contagious patients are marked with a bug icon during an outbreak). You are offered a timed containment contract — vaccinate every infected patient with your Nurses before the deadline to earn a cash-and-reputation bonus; fail, and reputation drops and the outbreak floods your treatment rooms. Any suitable illness can appear in epidemic form on later levels; the in-game flavour is handled per-level rather than per-illness, which is why the tables below don't carry a contagion column. Emergencies are the other batch-arrival mechanic: an ambulance delivers a group of patients with the same illness against a timer for a bonus reward.

## Level-specific and notable rarities

The **First seen** column above gives each illness's debut hospital; illnesses generally recur in later hospitals of the same career, but a few are effectively locked to specific places (per the wiki's hospital-appearance tables, which are incomplete on some pages — treat the list below as the well-attested cases):

- **Rock Star** (Psychiatry) — a Freddie Mercury homage; the wiki records it only at **Clockwise-before-Thyme**, largely via Psychiatry emergency events.
- **8-bitten** (Resolution Lab) — debuts at **Duckworth-upon-Bilge**; the Resolution Lab must be researched, so the illness only circulates in late-career hospitals (Grockle Bay onward).
- **Premature Mummification** (Cryptology) — debuts at **Sweaty Palms** in the Tropical region, where Cryptology first unlocks.
- **Animal Magnetism** (Pest Control) — tied to **Flemington**, where Pest Control becomes researchable.
- **Headcrabedness** (De-Lux Clinic) — a Half-Life crossover added free in the March 2019 *Open Surgery* update rather than at launch.
- **Frightheadedness** — a Halloween-event reskin of Lightheadedness (seasonal only; not a separate wiki entry, noted here for completeness from the GameFAQs guide).
- **DLC illnesses** appear only in their expansion's three (or so) hospitals — e.g. **Rantlers**, **Bloaty Dread** and the other Bigfoot ailments circulate only around Underlook Hotel, Swelbard and Roquefort Castle — though DLC owners will also meet them in Sandbox mode and some cross-over emergencies.
- **Speedy Recovery** illnesses (19 of them, e.g. **Frosted Tips**, **Piste Off**, **Hive Mind**) are built around the DLC's ambulance-dispatch mechanic and are fetched from off-site rather than walking in.
