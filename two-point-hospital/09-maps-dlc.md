# Two Point Hospital — DLC Hospitals: Per-Map Strategy

Covers all seven map-adding DLC expansions in release order. Groupings below are verified against the Two Point Hospital wiki: note that **Pointy Pass is a Speedy Recovery hospital, not Bigfoot** (Bigfoot's third map is Roquefort Castle), and "Everything Ville" / "Sandybox Hospital" are unfinished developer maps only reachable via the command console (they crash on load) — they belong to no DLC and are excluded here.

## 1. Bigfoot DLC (5 December 2018)

**Theme:** the snowy Pointy Mountains, hosted by VIP yeti Bartholomew F. Yeti. Adds 3 hospitals (World 6), 34 region-specific illnesses (9 visual) and 3 new treatment clinics: **Doghouse** (K9-Away machine, cures Barking Mad), **Urban Mythology** (Inner-City-Simulator, cures Metropolism), **Reanimation** (Cryomatic, cures Monster Mishmash). Unlocks after 1 star in Flemington, but bring Surgery and DNA Lab knowledge from Smogley/Melt Downs first — Bigfoot illnesses need them.

### Underlook Hotel
- **Setting/gimmick:** a much-maligned winter ski resort ("The Shining" pastiche). Target Challenge Mode: patients pay nothing; income comes from rotating hotel targets ($18k–80k each) plus a quarterly Performance Bonus (Index Challenge debut) that covers a percentage of running costs equal to your cure rate. Starts at hospital level 3 with only 22% reputation. Cold map — patients need warming (radiators).
- **Stars:** 1★ Cure 60 patients, 65% reputation, 3 marketing campaigns, cure 12 Barking Mad. 2★ Cure 100, 80% reputation, 5 campaigns, level-5 Doghouse. 3★ Cure 200, 95% reputation, 8 campaigns, 80% staff morale.
- **New here:** Barking Mad illness + Doghouse.
- **Strategy:** since patients don't pay, keep the build lean — small GP cluster, Pharmacy, Ward, Fracture Ward, Psychiatry, Doghouse — and let targets fund expansion. Always keep all three target slots progressing; prioritise the train/promote targets (train 4 staff = $55k) since training also feeds the cure-rate bonus. Build a Marketing room early and run campaigns continuously — three star tiers demand 3/5/8 completed campaigns and marketing is also the only realistic way to drag reputation from 22% to 95%. For the 3★ morale requirement: pay slightly above asking, staff room with pool table/arcade, and prompt breaks. Pitfall: overexpanding — every empty room is pure cost here because the Performance Bonus only covers wages/energy at your cure-rate percentage. Keep radiators up so queueing patients don't freeze and leave (lost cures hurt the bonus).

### Swelbard
- **Setting/gimmick:** the most remote town in the County; you rejuvenate the Swelbard Research Institute. Only level-1, skill-less recruits apply (Mitton University-style), but every completed training course pays a $7,500 grant. Star goals hinge on research projects (Urban Mythology room, then one-shot cash projects "Roachburger Scandal" and "Project Windsock" — a wink at the later Off the Grid map).
- **Stars:** 1★ Train 20 staff, 60% cure rate, level-5 Research room, complete Urban Mythology project. 2★ Train 30 staff, 75% cure rate, 2 staff with Research III, Roachburger Scandal project. 3★ Train 50 staff, 90% cure rate, cure 35 in Urban Mythology, Project Windsock.
- **New here:** Metropolism (Mechanical/Reptilian) + Urban Mythology room.
- **Strategy:** build two Training rooms immediately and keep them running non-stop — 50 trainings is the long pole, and the $7.5k-per-course grant means training is nearly self-funding. Build a large (level-5) Research room early, staff it with doctors you've given Research I–III, and chain the three projects; park spare research output on machine upgrades for the cure-rate goals. Hire cheap juniors in bulk (they're all juniors anyway), train each in one specialisation (GP's Office/Treatment/Ward), and use externally-hired trainers for skills nobody has. 90% cure rate demands upgraded machines and correct treatment-room skill matches — don't send level-1 staff into Surgery. Pitfall: neglecting diagnostics; cold-map illnesses here are obscure, so a General Diag + Cardio + DNA Lab chain keeps misdiagnoses from tanking the cure rate.

### Roquefort Castle
- **Setting/gimmick:** an aristocratic family's spare castle serving the mountain community — awkward castle layout and the new **Ghost Infestation** disaster, which periodically spawns swarms of ghosts (plot 16 is a hotspot). Starts at level 1 with $250k.
- **Stars:** 1★ 70% attractiveness, $1.5M hospital value, cure 150, capture 180 ghosts. 2★ Cure 300, $3M value, cure 25 Monster Mishmash, capture 300 ghosts. 3★ Cure 600, $5M value, 90% staff morale, 90% cure rate.
- **New here:** Monster Mishmash (only appearance) + Reanimation room.
- **Strategy:** hire a janitor squad where most have (or are trained in) **Ghost Capture** — infestations dump dozens of ghosts at once and un-captured ghosts scare patients into leaving. The value targets ($1.5M→$5M) are the real grind: buy every plot, fill rooms with expensive items, and upgrade every machine (upgrades add value and feed the 90% cure rate). Attractiveness 70% comes free if you decorate for value anyway — plants, gold statues, rugs in corridors. Build Reanimation early (Monster Mishmash appears only here; 25 cures is a 2★ gate) and market for it if patients run dry. For 600 cures, scale to multiple GP offices (4+), duplicate Pharmacy/Ward, and raise hospital level to lift patient caps. Pitfall: treating ghost capture as optional — during an infestation, pause hiring decisions and manually direct trained janitors; a hospital emptied by hauntings can't hit cure-count targets.

## 2. Pebberley Island DLC (18 March 2019)

**Theme:** a tropical island paradise with explorer VIP Wiggy Silverbottom hunting the fountain of eternal youth. 3 hospitals (World 7), 34 new illnesses, 3 new clinics: **Identification** (Face Stamp, cures Blank Look), **Escape Room** (Counterfeat, cures Wanderust), **Correcting Pool** (Autocue, cures Screwball). Introduces Plot Challenge Mode and Horde Challenge Mode, both reused by later DLCs.

### Pebberley Reef
- **Setting/gimmick:** hot coastal reef hospital battered by **frequent disasters** (typhoons/monsoons, epidemics, vomit waves, bug infestations). Janitor-centric: janitors can only be recruited at junior level with no skills, and initial objectives (sweep 100 litter, hire 3 janitors) gate your medical licence.
- **Stars:** 1★ 70% cure rate, train 12 janitors, cure 10 Blank Look, water 40 plants. 2★ 80% cure rate, hire 12 janitors, 75% attractiveness, upgrade 15 machines. 3★ 90% cure rate, train 20 janitors, sweep 150 litter, $2.5M hospital value.
- **New here:** Blank Look + Identification room.
- **Strategy:** run an oversized janitorial corps from the start — 12+ hires, with a dedicated Training room cycling them through Maintenance and Motivation (20 janitor trainings needed for 3★). Assign a couple purely to watering: the map is packed with plants and "water 40 plants" recurs. Upgrading 15 machines does double duty (2★ goal + cure rate + hospital value), so build a Research room or buy upgrades as soon as machines hit level requirements. Disasters hit often and hard: keep janitors idle-capacity high so machine repairs never queue during an earthquake, and stock hand sanitiser for epidemics. Litter and sick patients wreck attractiveness — extra bins everywhere plus decorative items push the 75%. Pitfall: hiring doctors aggressively while ignoring janitors; on this map an unrepaired machine during a storm cascades into explosions, lost rooms, and a collapsing cure rate.

### Overgrowth
- **Setting/gimmick:** jungle ruins being reclaimed from the vines. **Plot Challenge Mode debut**: money works normally, but the 25 plots can't be bought — each unlocks 10 days after you complete a chosen plot objective (up to three active at once).
- **Stars:** 1★ Own 10 plots. 2★ Own 18 plots. 3★ Own 25 plots. (That's the entire star list — everything else is instrumental.)
- **New here:** Wanderust + Escape Room.
- **Strategy:** the level is a checklist race. Always have three plot objectives running; when one is near completion, pre-read the next objectives and start positioning (e.g., if one asks for training or marketing, have those rooms built before you accept). Objectives range from cure counts to room prestige to staff goals — pick the three that overlap most with what your hospital does naturally, and sequence unlocks along the dependency chains shown on the plot map (some plots require owning specific neighbours). Since income is normal, run a compact profitable core (GP ×3, Pharmacy, Ward, Psychiatry, Escape Room) and expand rooms only when an objective demands it — you need plots, not size. The 10-day unlock delay means momentum matters: never let all three slots sit finished simultaneously. Pitfall: accepting three hard, slow objectives at once (e.g., multiple large cure-count goals) and stalling; mix one slow with two quick ones. Wanderust patients want to escape — the Escape Room and decent entertainment keep them from literally wandering off mid-queue.

### Topless Mountain
- **Setting/gimmick:** a volcano-top temple site where Wiggy's dig uncovers the Fountain of Eternal Youth as you clear waves. **Horde Challenge Mode debut**: patients arrive in discrete waves; every patient must be processed (cured, sent home, or quit) before a 7-day countdown starts the next wave. Wave 1 = 10 patients at a 55% required cure rate; +5 patients per wave, +5% cure floor per 10 waves (capped 70% after 3★). Curing 10 in a row grants 150% income while the streak holds. Starts with a big $400k balance for a reason. Endless after wave 30; wave 42 gives the "Life, the Universe and Everything" achievement.
- **Stars:** 1★ Complete Wave 10. 2★ Wave 20. 3★ Wave 30.
- **New here:** Screwball (only appearance) + Correcting Pool (arrives with wave 4).
- **Strategy:** use the quiet between waves to build ahead — you know wave sizes are coming, so by wave 15 you want 4–5 GP offices, doubled diagnostics (General Diag + Cardio + X-Ray/MEGA-Scan), and every treatment room the wave table lists before its debut wave. Staff costs run during downtime, so don't overhire early; instead train the staff you have (upgrades between waves are free time). Guard the cure streak: manually send hopeless (low-health, low-diagnosis) patients home rather than risk a failed cure breaking 150% income and dropping the wave cure rate below the floor. Watch energy/wage burn during countdowns — big idle payrolls between waves are the classic bankruptcy here. Pitfall: letting a giant late wave (60+ patients) hit a single reception/GP bottleneck; queue caps and extra receptionists keep processing inside the wave window.

## 3. Close Encounters DLC (29 August 2019)

**Theme:** desert conspiracy country (Roswell/Area 51 riff), hosted by "certainly-human" journalist VIP Charlatene Fishwrapper. 3 hospitals (World 8), 34 new illnesses, 3 new clinics: **Self-Assembly** (Character Creator, cures Flat-Packed), **Toad Hall** (Poly-Cog, cures Frogborne), **Personification** (Spin-Doctor, cures Lack of Humanity). Signature mechanic: disguised **alien infiltrators** clogging your queues.

### Goldpan
- **Setting/gimmick:** a dusty gold-rush mining town. Plot Challenge Mode returns (15-day plot unlock after objectives), combined with **Alien Infiltrators**: fake patients that cycle through diagnosis rooms forever, never pay, and jump queues. Spot them by cloak-flickers, taking out bin bags, skipping Reception/GP (0% diagnosis in a diagnosis queue), name/appearance changes, or idle sonic-screwdriver animations — then send them home from the patient panel.
- **Stars:** 1★ Own 5 plots, expose 3 infiltrators. 2★ Own 10 plots, expose 5. 3★ Own 15 plots, expose 8.
- **New here:** Flat-Packed + Self-Assembly room.
- **Strategy:** same cadence as Overgrowth — keep three plot objectives always active and pick overlapping ones — plus a periodic infiltrator sweep: open the patient list and sort/scan diagnosis queues for anyone at 0% diagnosis who has visited multiple diagnosis rooms; watching a suspect for a few seconds usually catches the flicker. Exposing only 8 total across three stars is generous, so don't obsess, but DO evict every infiltrator you find — each one wastes diagnosis throughput you're paying for. Run lean and profitable (normal economy), build Self-Assembly early for Flat-Packed cures, and put entertainment near diagnosis queues since real patients wait longer behind fakes. Pitfall: manually sending a suspected infiltrator to treatment — they won't pay and you may kill a real patient; always use "send home" on suspects instead.

### Camouflage Falls
- **Setting/gimmick:** lush conspiracy-tourism falls. **Horde Challenge, hard mode**: waves now carry objectives that must be completed *before the last patient of the wave is processed*, or the wave repeats. 1 objective for waves 1–10, 2 for 11–20, 3 for 21–30; patient counts swing wildly (5–200). 10-day countdown between waves; 10-cure streak again gives 150% income. After wave 30 the level continues with randomized repeat waves.
- **Stars:** 1★ Complete Wave 10. 2★ Wave 20. 3★ Wave 30.
- **New here:** Frogborne + Toad Hall (arrives wave 7).
- **Strategy:** the wave-repeat rule inverts Topless Mountain logic: sometimes you must *slow down* patient processing to finish objectives first. Read each wave's objectives the moment it starts; if an objective is slow (train staff, complete marketing, upgrade machines), immediately start it and, if patients are running out, deliberately throttle — close a treatment room door or hold the last patients in queue until the objective completes. Between waves, pre-build for known illness debuts (Toad Hall before wave 7) and pre-queue training courses you can trigger during the wave. Keep a cash cushion for objective purchases (posters, items, marketing) demanded mid-wave. Small waves (5 patients) with an objective are the trap — five patients process fast, so start the objective within seconds. Pitfall: hiring for peak 200-patient waves and drowning in wages during 5-patient waves; prefer training and machine upgrades (permanent, no upkeep) over headcount.

### Chasm 24
- **Setting/gimmick:** a snowbound secret military facility over a chasm. Every purchased plot contains a **disaster stabiliser** (suppressing a specific disaster — epidemics, earthquakes, etc.) whose maintenance gauge constantly drains; hitting zero unleashes the disaster, which damages machinery *including other stabilisers*, chain-reacting fast. Only **Robo-Janitors** (supplied with certain plots) can maintain stabilisers. Alien infiltrators also appear (no objective, but evict them). Normal economy, cold map.
- **Stars:** 1★ 70% cure rate, own 4 plots, complete Personification research, cure 10 in Personification. 2★ 80% cure rate, own 8 plots, cure 25 in Personification, 3,000 research points. 3★ 90% cure rate, 5,000 research points, cure 50 in Personification, own 12 plots.
- **New here:** Lack of Humanity (only appearance) + Personification room; Robo-Janitors.
- **Strategy:** expand deliberately — each new plot adds a stabiliser liability, so before buying, check whether the plot also grants a Robo-Janitor (the wiki plot table marks these) and keep your Robo-to-stabiliser ratio healthy. Consider disabling Robo-Janitors' generic tasks so they focus purely on machine/stabiliser maintenance. Build a big Research room early: two star tiers demand raw research points, and the Personification project is your 1★ gate — staff it with Research-trained doctors permanently. Personification cures (10/25/50) mean Lack of Humanity patients are precious: prioritise their diagnosis and never send them home. A radiator-warmed, upgrade-heavy compact hospital hits the 90% cure rate more safely than a sprawling one with distant stabilisers. Pitfall: buying plots in a burst before Robo coverage catches up — one earthquake then knocks out three stabilisers and the hospital burns; also note the Robo-Kit (build extra Robo-Janitors) only unlocks at 3★, so you can't buy your way out on the first playthrough.

## 4. Off the Grid DLC (25 March 2020)

**Theme:** eco-friendly healthcare for the Department of Green Things, hosted by VIP Flanolin Onionsack (creature of politician Tabitha Windsock). 3 hospitals (World 9), 35 new illnesses (9 visual), new rooms: **Woodwork** (cures Woodworms), **Farmacology** (cures Distrawed), **Herb Garden** (new plant-themed multi-illness ward), **Tech Support** (Next-Generator, cures Glitchy Patch). Gimmick arc: index-funded economy → machine-free sustenance → player-managed green power.

### Wanderoff
- **Setting/gimmick:** a back-to-nature retreat. **Wellness Index** (expanded Index Challenge): normal income is slashed; every 3 months Holistix scores you on six categories — cure rate, staff happiness, patient happiness, attractiveness, hygiene, reputation — and covers 50–120% of wages+energy for the next quarter. Above 100%, hiring staff literally makes money.
- **Stars:** 1★ 70% staff morale, 70% cure rate, cure 8 in Woodwork. 2★ 80% morale, 80% cure rate, train 20 staff, upgrade 7 machines. 3★ 90% morale, 90% cure rate, 75% attractiveness, cure 75 patients.
- **New here:** Woodworms + Woodwork room.
- **Strategy:** everything you do should feed an index category. Balanced play wins: pay staff at or above expectations, big staff room, prompt breaks (morale is a star goal *and* an index line); food/drink/entertainment along queues (patient happiness); bins, plants, décor (attractiveness); sanitiser and stocked toilets (hygiene); a Marketing room ticking over (reputation). Because the index covers running costs, front-load hiring and training once your score is high — a 110–120% quarter is free staff. Cure 75 patients total is trivial; the 90/90 morale/cure-rate pair is the 3★ wall, so upgrade machines (also a 2★ goal) and keep specialists in matching rooms. Pitfall: a single bad quarter (e.g., an epidemic tanking hygiene/happiness) drops coverage to ~50% and can bankrupt an over-hired hospital — keep a two-quarter cash reserve and fix index dips immediately after each assessment letter.

### Old Newpoint
- **Setting/gimmick:** a historic town gone fully green — **no vending machines, kiosks, drink fountains or shops** for sustenance. Only self-sustaining dispensers (Rainwater, Carrots, Lemons, Tomatoes — the latter two unlock during the level) may feed corridors; they need no janitor restock and recharge over time. The Café and in-room dispensers (coffee makers etc.) are still allowed. Expansion is by **buildings** (separate structures), not plots.
- **Stars:** 1★ Own 4 buildings, cure 8 in Farmacology, feed 250 people, place 25 carrots/lemons/tomatoes/rainwater. 2★ Own 7 buildings, cure 25 in Herb Garden, feed 500, 70% cure rate. 3★ Own 10 buildings, cure 50 in Herb Garden, feed 1,000, 80% cure rate.
- **New here:** Distrawed + Farmacology; debut of the Herb Garden multi-illness room.
- **Strategy:** carpet-bomb corridors with sustainable dispensers well past the "place 25" goal — the feed-1,000 counter only climbs when people actually eat/drink from them, so put clusters at every queue and waiting area and add a Café as backup (hungry patients lose happiness fast when four carrots are recharging). Build the Herb Garden early and make it big: 50 Herb Garden cures is the 3★ long pole, and it works like a Ward, so one large well-staffed garden with trained nurses beats two small ones. Buy buildings steadily — new buildings also give you room to duplicate GP/diagnostics for the 80% cure rate. Farmacology handles Distrawed; get its 8 cures done in year one. Pitfall: forgetting dispensers recharge slowly — if the "feed" counters stall, you under-placed; sustainable items are cheap, so redundancy costs almost nothing.

### Windsock
- **Setting/gimmick:** Tabitha Windsock's flagship green city. **Energy Challenge Mode**: the hospital's electricity is player-generated. Green generators are bought like plots — solar arrays, wind turbines, hydro dams (best value: the small triangular solar at $1,000/unit; the big circular array gives 25 units for $60k) — up to 119 total units. Energy is spent on three sliders: keeping rooms open, patient flow rate, and staff-applicant rate.
- **Stars:** 1★ Generate 25 energy units, upgrade 5 machines, 1,200 research points, cure 8 in Tech Support. 2★ 50 units, upgrade 10 machines, promote 15 staff, 70% cure rate. 3★ 90 units, upgrade 15 machines, 80% staff morale, 85% cure rate.
- **New here:** Glitchy Patch (only appearance) + Tech Support room.
- **Strategy:** buy generators by cost-per-unit, cheapest first (small triangular solar → circular array → hydro dams → wind turbines), and treat the 25/50/90-unit goals as your expansion schedule — don't outbuild your power budget, because underpowered rooms close. Early on, weight energy toward room uptime; shift surplus into patient flow once treatment capacity exists, and into applicant rate only when hiring. A Research room does triple duty here: research points are a 1★ goal, and the 15 machine upgrades for 3★ need research output — keep it permanently staffed. Promote 15 staff (2★) means hiring early so staff level up in time; promotions also lift morale toward the 80% goal. Pitfall: buying the flashy big-ticket generators first and running out of cash for rooms — the map has finite generators, so efficiency order matters; and never let a machine-heavy wing brown-out mid-treatment, which kills cure rate and patients alike.

## 5. Culture Shock DLC (20 October 2020)

**Theme:** arts and celebrity culture, hosted by VIP Zara Fitzpocket. 3 hospitals (World 10), 36 new illnesses (12 visual), 3 new clinics: **Danger Zone** (Crash Course, cures Stunt Trouble), **Wash Pit** (Rub-O-Dub Tub, cures Soiled Self), **War Room** (Meltn' Mould, cures Private Parts).

### Plywood Studios
- **Setting/gimmick:** you run the on-set hospital of a TV medical drama. Celebrity "doctor" **Roderick Cushion** starts as your only staffer: he can't be assigned rooms (wanders into any room he's qualified for), abandons queues, breaks in his private trailer, draws a huge salary, and can't be fired — yet star goals require HIM to diagnose and cure patients. Target Challenge returns as compulsory "Producer Ideas" targets paid *on top of* normal income (some absurd: kill 3 patients $40k, let a machine explode $60k).
- **Stars:** 1★ Complete 4 marketing campaigns, Roderick diagnoses 3 patients, cure 6 Stunt Trouble. 2★ 85% reputation, 80% staff morale, 75% hygiene, Roderick cures 16. 3★ 95% reputation, cure 25 Stunt Trouble, Roderick cures 20.
- **New here:** Stunt Trouble + Danger Zone.
- **Strategy:** herd Roderick by construction: to farm his cures, keep few treatment rooms he's qualified for open near his trailer, and pick him up and drop him into a queued treatment room whenever he strays (you can manually place him, just not assign him). Training him (level 5 = achievement) widens where he's useful. Build the Danger Zone early — Stunt Trouble is common on a stunt-filled set and 25 cures gate 3★. Producer Ideas can't be declined, so budget for the weird ones; "kill off a patient" targets can be left to complete naturally (or ignore morality and pull a doctor mid-surgery — reputation hit is small next to 95% rep from constant marketing). Marketing room from day one: 4 campaigns for 1★ and reputation goals for 2★/3★. Pitfall: letting Roderick idle in a diagnosis room while cure targets stall — check on him every in-game month; his salary is sunk cost either way, so extract value.

### Mudbury Festival
- **Setting/gimmick:** a mud-soaked music-festival field hospital. Horde Challenge, objective-based (Camouflage Falls rules): waves of 20–140 patients, 1–3 objectives per wave that must complete before the last patient is processed or the wave repeats; 8-day countdown between waves; 10-cure streak = 150% income. Filth is everywhere — hygiene objectives suit the setting.
- **Stars:** 1★ Complete Wave 6. 2★ Wave 12. 3★ Wave 18. (18 unique waves, then randomized repeats.)
- **New here:** Soiled Self + Wash Pit (arrives wave 4).
- **Strategy:** shorter than the other horde maps but denser: only 6 waves per star and the 8-day gap is tight, so pre-build during countdowns using the wave table (Wash Pit before wave 4). Janitors matter unusually much — mud, litter and Soiled Self patients wreck hygiene, and hygiene/cleaning objectives recur; hire a big janitor team early and add extra toilets and sanitiser. Apply the throttle trick from Camouflage Falls: if a slow objective (training, marketing) risks missing the wave end, stall the last patients in a queue until it completes. Watch the swing from 20-patient to 140-patient waves — build reception/GP for the peak, but staff treatment rooms with multi-purpose (well-trained, movable) doctors instead of specialists sitting idle in small waves. Pitfall: mid-wave objective purchases when cash is thin after over-building; keep ~$50k liquid every wave for whatever the objective demands.

### Fitzpocket Academy
- **Setting/gimmick:** the Fitzpockets' elite arts academy, temperate climate. Index Challenge returns as the **VIBE system** (quarterly, covers 50–120% of wages+energy) scoring cure rate, patient happiness, patient health, attractiveness, hygiene, and **room prestige**. Plus **paparazzi** infiltrators (alien-infiltrator mechanic reskinned) sneaking around for celebrity photos — spot camera flashes and bin-rummaging, send them home. A parade of returning VIPs visits as "Zara's Guests" and must be impressed.
- **Stars:** 1★ Impress 2 VIPs, expose 8 paparazzi, 75% cure rate. 2★ Impress 5 VIPs, 80% attractiveness, cure 12 Private Parts, 85% cure rate. 3★ Impress 10 VIPs, expose 25 paparazzi, 90% cure rate.
- **New here:** Private Parts (only appearance) + War Room.
- **Strategy:** VIP-proof the hospital permanently rather than scrambling per visit: max room prestige (which VIBE also pays for) by upsizing rooms and filling them with high-prestige items, keep corridors decorated and spotless, and staff happy — then every VIP tour lands well. Impressing 10 VIPs takes many visits, so accept every VIP event. Paparazzi: 25 exposures is a real number — do a patient-list sweep whenever you see a flash; they never visit GP, so anyone bouncing between diagnosis rooms at 0% is suspect. Build the War Room early for the 12 Private Parts cures (this map only). The 90% cure rate + VIBE synergy rewards machine upgrades and specialist training; with a 110%+ VIBE score, over-hiring becomes profitable, so scale staff aggressively once prestige categories are green. Pitfall: chasing VIP visits with a mediocre hospital — a failed visit wastes the opportunity; fix VIBE categories first, VIP counts follow.

## 6. A Stitch in Time DLC (23 February 2021)

**Theme:** time travel with VIP inventor Captain Yesterday. 3 hospitals (World 11), 34 new illnesses (13 visual) drawn from four eras (Prehistoric, Medieval, Modern, Future). Uniquely, it adds **no new cure clinics** — its illnesses route to existing rooms (e.g., Reptile Dysfunction/Rock Star → Psychiatry, Woolly Man-Mouth → DNA Lab) — instead adding two temporal facilities: **Time Portals** (patient source; each raises Spacetime Dilation ~8.33%, increasing patient volume and illness variety) and **Speed Dating** (Yesterizer machine, for returning patients to their eras). New disaster: Time Storm. Unlocks off Mitton University rather than Flemington.

### Clockwise-upon-Thyme
- **Setting/gimmick:** a Medieval-era castle town hospital treating Medieval + Modern patients. One natural Time Portal at start; each of the additional plots contains another (8 total, 66% max Dilation until you later return with artificial portals researched in Clockwise-before-Thyme). Cold map, small $120k start, level 2.
- **Stars:** 1★ Own 4 buildings, cure 25 patients from Time Portals, cure 10 Reptile Dysfunction. 2★ Cure 50 portal patients, 80% attractiveness, upgrade 4 machines. 3★ Cure 75 portal patients, 85% cure rate, 85% staff morale.
- **New rooms/illnesses:** Time Portals mechanic; era illnesses like Reptile Dysfunction (Psychiatry), Jester Infection, Beheadedness. Bring Fracture Ward and the researched Injection Room (needed for the illness roster).
- **Strategy:** portal patients are the currency of every star tier, so buy plots early — each plot = another portal = more Dilation = more (and stranger) patients. But pace Dilation against capacity: every portal raises patient inflow, so add a GP office and a diagnostic room per couple of portals. Reptile Dysfunction needs Psychiatry: train 2–3 psychiatrists before pushing Dilation. Attractiveness 80% and morale 85% are standard décor/pay/staff-room work; upgrade 4 machines rides on a small Research room or spare cash. With only $120k start, open minimal (GP, General Diag, Pharmacy, Psychiatry) and let portal volume fund growth. Pitfall: maxing portals early and drowning a two-GP hospital in medieval peasants — queue collapse tanks the 85% cure rate; expand portals last, rooms first.

### Clockwise-before-Thyme
- **Setting/gimmick:** the Prehistoric era (hot, volcanic). Only one natural portal — all others must be **researched and built by you** (artificial Time Portals, placed like items, movable), directly controlling Spacetime Dilation. First map with all four eras' illnesses. From 1★, Captain Yesterday adds hygiene-flavoured mini-targets ($5,000 each, on top of normal income). Needs Surgery (from Smogley) for its roster.
- **Stars:** 1★ Complete Time Portal research, place 3 Time Portals, hospital level 7, cure 6 Rock Star. 2★ Cure 50 portal patients, place 6 portals, hospital level 11, cure 10 Woolly Man-Mouth. 3★ Cure 75 portal patients, 85% cure rate, complete Time Travel research project.
- **New rooms/illnesses:** artificial Time Portal (buildable); Rock Star (Psychiatry), Woolly Man-Mouth (DNA Lab).
- **Strategy:** Research room first — the Time Portal project gates 1★ and the Time Travel project gates 3★, so keep Research-trained doctors employed all level. Hospital level 7/11 targets reward breadth: more rooms, more items, more staff all raise hospital level, so this is the map to build generously. Place portals where corridors can absorb the spawns (near reception/diagnostics, not dead ends), and since they're movable, relocate them as you rebuild. Psychiatry (Rock Star) and DNA Lab (Woolly Man-Mouth) need trained specialists early. The hygiene targets are small but free — keep janitors ahead of mess and collect. Pitfall: forgetting the artificial portal tech carries back to Clockwise-upon-Thyme — if you shelved that map short of 3★, return after this research to push its Dilation and portal-cure counts.

### Clockwise-above-Thyme
- **Setting/gimmick:** the Future era, cold and neon. Adds **Yesterization**: cured Prehistoric/Medieval/Modern patients don't leave — they queue for the **Speed Dating** room, where an Assistant trained in the Yesterization skill operates the Yesterizer to portal them home. No Speed Dating room (or zero happiness) = rage quit, losing the "sent back" credit. Needs the Recurvery Room (from Grockle Bay).
- **Stars:** 1★ Train an Assistant in Yesterization, cure 5 Artificial Intelligence, send back 30 Lost in Time patients. 2★ Send back 60, train 2 Assistants in Yesterization II, 80% reputation. 3★ Send back & cure 75, train 3 Assistants in Yesterization III, 80% reputation, entertain 999 patients.
- **New rooms/illnesses:** Speed Dating room + Yesterizer; Artificial Intelligence (Psychiatry), Futurism, Bionic Plague.
- **Strategy:** treat Speed Dating as a second reception: build it early, large, and consider two rooms once Dilation rises, each with a Yesterization-trained Assistant (you need 3 trained to III anyway — start those long training chains immediately, they're the slowest 3★ item). Keep patient happiness high end-to-end (entertainment items also tick the "entertain 999 patients" counter — saturate corridors with TVs, arcade machines, VR) because an unhappy cured patient rage-quits instead of Yesterizing. AI illness → Psychiatry again; train psychiatrists. Reputation 80% via marketing and word of mouth. Pitfall: a Speed Dating bottleneck — if the queue backs up, cured patients time out and your send-back counters stall; monitor its queue like you would GP, and pull the Assistant off breaks during rushes (or staff a substitute).

## 7. Speedy Recovery DLC (15 March 2022)

**Theme:** emergency response. 3 hospitals (World 12) in revisited regions (no prior DLC required), 19 new illnesses (6 visual), 3 new clinics: **Cloud Computing** (Storm Drain, cures Under The Weather), **Wax Works** (Honey Trap, cures Hive Mind), **Powder Room** (Flake Cannon, cures Snow Problem). Core mechanic: an **ambulance fleet** — road (Compliant Colin, Pantomobile, Big Healer) and air (Relicopter, Airloovator, Feather Balloon) — dispatched from Ambulance Stations via the "Two Two Point Nav" map to emergencies (major = red, focused illnesses, 20 patients; minor = yellow, mixed; severity 1–5 sets the death timer; icons mark drive-to vs fly-to scenes). Engineer **Gretchen Gearbox** joins your staff to research/upgrade/repair vehicles. Two rival foundations per map compete on a league table (patients collected, cure rate, death rate, reputation...) that star goals reference. Recommended prep: Surgery, DNA Lab, and Sweaty Palms progress.

### Ailing
- **Setting/gimmick:** a cold mountain town (Pointy Mountains revisited) where the dispatch system debuts. Road ambulances only; rivals are Bungle Technologies and Holistix. Starts level 3, 22% reputation.
- **Stars:** 1★ Collect 100 emergency patients, cure 8 Under The Weather, place 3 Compliant Colins. 2★ Complete Pantomobile and Big Healer research, cure 100 emergency patients, place 1st in patients collected. 3★ Place 1st in emergency cure rate, 70% cure rate on emergency patients, build 3 Ambulance Stations, collect 200 emergency patients, 90% reputation.
- **New here:** Under The Weather + Cloud Computing; ambulance mechanic itself.
- **Strategy:** dispatch discipline beats hospital size here. Answer every viable emergency immediately — patients on scene die on a visible timer, and rivals race you to them; prefer high-severity majors your treatment rooms can actually cure (majors focus on 1–2 illnesses, so check the illness before committing). Build the extra Ambulance Stations early (3 needed for 3★) and fill them: more vehicles = parallel responses = league-table dominance in "collected." Research Pantomobile (big capacity) and Big Healer (off-road) as soon as Gretchen allows — both are 2★ gates and better tools. Ambulances auto-shuttle until a scene is cleared; don't cancel assignments mid-scene. Inside the hospital, emergency intakes arrive in batches of the same illness — build/expand the specific treatment room *before* the convoy returns, and keep Cloud Computing staffed (Under The Weather is common locally). The 70% emergency cure rate means don't scoop patients you can't treat. Pitfall: ignoring vehicle repair — broken-down ambulances strand patients; keep Gretchen's maintenance current and stations staffed.

### Betts Shore
- **Setting/gimmick:** a sunny coastal resort (Pebberley Island revisited) adding **air rescue**: the Relicopter (needs staff trained in the new Flying skill) reaches sea and island emergencies road vehicles can't. Rivals: The Orb Foundation (also airborne) and Bungle Technologies (ground).
- **Stars:** 1★ Train 3 staff in Flying, place 3 Relicopters, cure 8 Hive Mind. 2★ Complete "Upgrade: Wax Works" research, hold at least 2nd in emergency reputation for 3 months, upgrade 10 machines, 95% reputation. 3★ Complete "Advanced Upgrade: Wax Works" research, $3M hospital value, hold 1st in emergency reputation for 3 months, hold 1st in patients collected for 3 months.
- **New here:** Hive Mind + Wax Works.
- **Strategy:** stand up the air wing first — train 3+ staff in Flying and field all 3 Relicopters quickly, because sea emergencies are where The Orb Foundation feeds; contesting them is how you climb the reputation table. The "hold 1st for 3 consecutive months" goals punish lapses: keep at least one ambulance idle-ready at all times so no emergency window goes unanswered, and split coverage (road units on land scenes, helicopters reserved for fly-to icons). The two Wax Works research projects are hard star gates — permanent Research room with dedicated staff, and do the 10 machine upgrades from the same pipeline (they also pump the $3M hospital value along with plots and décor). Reputation 95% needs marketing plus consistently rescued-and-cured patients (deaths at scenes hurt emergency reputation). Pitfall: letting a single missed month reset a 3-month streak at 90% done — watch the league table monthly and over-commit vehicles in the final stretch.

### Pointy Pass
- **Setting/gimmick:** a snowbound mountain pass with the DLC's twist ending: **no walk-in patients at all** — Reception and GP's Offices are pointless; every patient is fetched from emergencies. Final two ambulances unlock (Airloovator, Feather Balloon); rivals Holistix and Jumbo MegaCorp run both road and air fleets.
- **Stars:** 1★ Complete Powder Room research, build 6 ambulances, cure 8 Snow Problem, collect 100 emergency patients, hold at least 2nd in patients collected for 3 months. 2★ Upgrade 20 machines, hold at least 2nd in emergency reputation for 3 months, cure 100 emergency patients. 3★ Hold 1st for 3 months in ALL of: patients collected, cure rate, death rate, cure-failure rate, and reputation.
- **New here:** Snow Problem (only appearance) + Powder Room.
- **Strategy:** rebuild your mental template: skip reception/GP entirely and spend that footprint on diagnostics and treatment rooms — emergency patients arrive knowing roughly what's wrong, in same-illness batches, so wide treatment coverage (including Powder Room, researched first for 1★) matters more than triage. Field the full six-vehicle fleet early (a 1★ goal anyway) mixing road and air so no emergency type is out of reach against two full-spectrum rivals. The 3★ five-category sweep is the hardest goal in the DLC: cure rate/failure/death columns mean *selective* scooping (only take illnesses you treat well), upgraded machines (20 upgrades is a 2★ gate that directly feeds cure stats), and top-tier specialists; the collection column means never letting vehicles idle. Since patient inflow is entirely dispatch-driven, income stalls whenever your fleet does — repair fast, and grab minor emergencies between majors to keep beds full. Pitfall: hospital-first habits — an empty gorgeous hospital earns nothing here; the dispatch map IS the game.
